# Real J&J suppliers — verified from SEC filings

**Purpose:** the analysis runs on California procurement data, but the strategic-supplier story should rest on real J&J relationships. These come from the suppliers' own annual reports (10-K) filed with the SEC, found by full-text search (2023–2026) and then read to confirm the **direction** of each relationship.

**Result:** of 9 candidates, **4 are confirmed J&J suppliers** (one of them former), **1 is a collaboration partner**, **2 are the reverse** (J&J supplies *them*), and **2 could not be confirmed**.

## Confirmed suppliers and what they show

| Company | What it supplies to J&J | Agreement and status | Kraljic quadrant | What it shows for the rationalization |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Alkermes plc** (ALKS) | Exclusive manufacturer of RISPERDAL CONSTA (long-acting injectable) at its Wilmington, Ohio plant, including the microspheres; Janssen packages and sells it | Manufacturing and supply agreement plus license agreements; Janssen can end supply on 6 months' notice (royalty then rises from 2.5% to 5%). Janssen collaborations = ~9% of Alkermes' 2025 revenue | **Strategic**: sole source, regulated, patient-critical | **Rule R1/R2 in practice:** a sole-source regulated manufacturer is never cut. It gets a partnership MOU on top of the supply and quality agreements. |
| **MeiraGTx** (MGTX) | Manufactures and supplies J&J's RPGR gene therapy (bota-vec) | Supply agreement from Dec 2023: 4-year term, J&J may extend to a 5th year; signed alongside J&J buying the program ($65M upfront + up to $350M contingent) | **Strategic / bottleneck**: specialised gene-therapy manufacturing, single source | A long-term supply agreement fits better than a tender. The fixed term with extension option matches the bottleneck template. |
| **Mersana Therapeutics** (MRSN) | Clinical manufacturing services for a J&J antibody-drug conjugate | Clinical supply agreement, Aug 2024; J&J prepays; J&J can request technology transfer of the bioconjugation and manufacturing process | **Bottleneck**: niche capability, low spend today | The **technology-transfer clause** is the second-source plan from the bottleneck template, written into a real contract. |
| **Emergent BioSolutions** (EBS) — *former* | Drug substance for J&J's COVID-19 vaccine at its Bayview (Baltimore) plant, contracted 2020 | J&J ended the contract in 2022, citing GMP breaches; dispute settled in 2024 with J&J paying Emergent $50M | Was **strategic**: concentrated, regulated CDMO | **Why the guardrails matter:** concentrating a critical product on one manufacturer without a qualified second source caused a supply failure. The bottleneck/strategic rules (quality agreement, change control, second source) exist for this case. |

## How this changes the story

1. **The strategic tier is real and concentrated.** J&J depends on single external manufacturers for specific products (Alkermes for RISPERDAL CONSTA, MeiraGTx for its RPGR gene therapy). These suppliers sit in the 2% of suppliers that hold most of the value, and the plan keeps them.
2. **Emergent is the counter-example.** It shows the cost of concentration without a qualified second source: a critical product was disrupted and the dispute took two years to settle. This supports rules R1–R2 and the bottleneck template's second-source roadmap.
3. **Real contracts already use the clauses in our templates:** termination notice periods (Alkermes), fixed terms with extension options (MeiraGTx), and technology transfer (Mersana).
